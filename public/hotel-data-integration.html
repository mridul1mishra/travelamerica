Weights and calculation

Total = Σ (known dimension score ÷ 4 × weight) ÷ Σ (known weights) × 100. Each dimension is scored 0–4. Unknown remains null and contributes neither points nor weight. A dimension is included only with a score, an evidence note and an official source. Partial scores and tiers are marked Provisional and show the percentage of the original weight supported by evidence. Complete assessments use all 100% of the weight.

Standard: below 50. Premium: 50 to below 75. Luxury: 75–100, with evidenced rooms and guest service each scoring at least 3/4. A score of 75 or more without both Luxury requirements produces Premium. Thresholds use the unrounded total. Partial scores can change when missing evidence becomes available.

Assess the regular property-wide offering. Suite-only or special-floor benefits do not upgrade the whole hotel. Separately listed hotel-within-a-hotel inventory may be assessed separately.

The agreed model uses five dimensions and fixed tier thresholds. Ratings, price, brand and marketing language are not scoring substitutes. A facility being unmentioned does not prove it is absent.

Approved tier definitions and review method

Standard: Comfortable accommodation with essential amenities and a simpler service offering.

Premium: Upscale accommodation with higher-quality rooms, design, and a broader service offering.

Luxury: Exceptional accommodation with consistently elevated, personalized service and high-end facilities.

Assess property-wide room quality, design, service and facilities. Special suite/floor benefits are identified. Price, guest review scores, brand, star counts and the word “luxury” alone do not determine a tier. Standard does not mean low quality or low price. No target distribution was imposed.
